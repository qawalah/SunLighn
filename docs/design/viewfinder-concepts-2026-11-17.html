SunWhere · viewfinder · two concepts · Nov 17

The viewfinder as a Braun instrument

The same shot in both: a 100 m tower 1.5 km away, the sun just on its top at 4:12 PM, through a 200 mm lens. The sun is drawn at its real size and height (4.1° up), so the pictures are to scale.

A Instrument

A handheld meter, after the ET66 calculator and the T3 radio: a graphite case, the scene in an inset screen, a readout under it, a scale and round keys.

Subjects: solid pictograms, lit on the sun's side

  • Scene in a screen. Compass degrees along its top, heights down its side, the lens and − + on the land.
  • Readout under it: the time in large figures, the day and the light, three numbers (height, bearing, distance).
  • Scale for time: a straight ruler with a yellow needle; the stops (Lined up, Sunset) are pins on it, the light names its stretches.
  • Keys are round and dark with their names under; one colour only, the body's.

B Poster and clock

An Ulm school poster with a Braun wall clock on it: the scene fills the screen in flat planes of colour, the time is set by turning a dial.

Subjects: line drawings, their top marked in yellow

  • Full screen. The sky is four flat bands of the hour's colour, the land one plane; nothing is boxed.
  • The time is the headline, top left, with the day, the light and where the sun is under it.
  • A clock dial replaces the ruler: turn it for time. The yellow hand is now, the orange dot is Lined up, the arcs are golden and blue hour.
  • Keys are plain icons with names under, no rings; the lens is a big number with − +.

Reply A or B, or mix them (for example A's case with B's subjects, or B with A's ruler instead of the dial).

Either way the keys, the words and Back stay as they are; AR (Compass with the camera) keeps the camera picture behind the same readout and keys. In Build the shot the subjects change to the chosen set too.
