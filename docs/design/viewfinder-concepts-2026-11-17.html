Two viewfinders for SunWhere

Both answer first, as you asked: when the sun is lined up, then the picture, then the time control and the keys you use (lens, Compass, Move, Height, Turn). Braun comes through the type, the colour, how dense it is and one signature detail each. The controls stay ordinary Android ones. The shot in both: a 100 m tower 1.5 km away, the sun on its top at 4:12 PM, 200 mm lens, drawn to scale.

AAnswer band

A band in the body's colour (orange for the sun, violet for the moon) holds the answer. The picture sits under it, then the time scale and the keys.

Reads first
Lined up at 4:12 PM, the day and the light, then height, bearing and where to stand on one line.
Time
A tuning scale like a Braun radio's: the light's stretches are its bands, Lined up and Sunset are stations, and the yellow needle is the time on screen. Drag to tune.
Signature
The tuning scale.
Quiet
The picture keeps only the sun, its path with two times marked, the ring where it lines up, and compass degrees and heights at the edges.

BCamera

Laid out like the phone's own camera: the picture fills the screen, the answer sits over the sky, and the controls sit on the land at the bottom.

Reads first
The time, large, over the sky; Lined up in orange under it, then the day, the light and the numbers.
Time
Drag the sky, or the slider over the keys. The orange key jumps back to the moment.
Signature
One coloured key, like the = key on Braun's ET66 calculator: a round orange Lined up key where a camera's shutter would be. Filled when you're at the moment; a ring with "in 7 min" when you're not.
Lens
A printed scale of focal lengths (24 to 400 mm) with a yellow mark; tap a length or pinch.

Subjects

Either set works with either layout. Against the sky a subject is a dark shape; both sets light the edge on the sun's side, as a camera sees it.

IPictograms

Built like Otl Aicher's Ulm pictograms: circles and straight edges at 45° and 90° only, solid, readable at any size.

IIMeasured

Drawn to the proportions the app works with (a tower is thin, a peak is wide), with a dimension line giving the height used.
