# SunLighn audit, 2026-11-14 (version 1.7.0)

How it was done: the real screens were run (Robolectric, `ScreensTest`, pictures in `app/build/screens/`), every
key pressed, and all of `MainActivity`, `FrameView`, `DayChart`, `Trip`, `Tutorial` and `SettingsSheet` read.
No phone was available, so touch feel (drag, pinch) is judged from the code.

Each finding has a mark: **[ ]** open, **[x] version** when fixed.

## The short version

The maths is good and the idea is right (give the answer, not a sun path). The trouble is that seven releases
each bolted a new feature onto one bottom card. The card now holds three flows, three views, two unrelated chip
rows that look the same, and a viewfinder squeezed into a box the size of a stamp. Nothing tells a new user which
of the two points they are placing, what a gesture will do, or where the answer is. The five worst problems:

1. **One card does everything.** Steps, answer, Day chart, Frame, Where to stand (twice, in two different
   forms), all in a fixed card that cannot be pulled up, pushed down or put away. It covers the map exactly
   when you need the map, and is too small exactly when you need the view.
2. **Chips mean four different things.** "Behind subject / Behind you" (a setting), "Times / Day / Frame"
   (tabs), "Evening sun / Moonrise…" (a filter), "100 m / 3 km" (a value) and "35 mm" (a lens) are the same
   pill. Two rows of identical pills sit on top of each other at the head of the card.
3. **The map taps are a guessing game.** The first tap is You, the second is Subject, and the third silently
   moves whichever point is nearer. There is no "you are placing: Subject" sign, no long-press, no way to
   place a point precisely, no search for a place, and the markers do not look draggable.
4. **The viewfinder does not show a photo.** See its own section below.
5. **The answer is small.** The thing the app exists for ("Tomorrow · 3:19 PM") is 16 sp text in a list row,
   smaller than the bearing "230° SW · 451 m" above it, which most users do not need at all.

## Screens

### Map (start, no points)
- [x] 2.0.0 The card says "Tap the map where you will stand" and offers "Use my location" and "Pick the object
  first". Most people know their subject, not their standing spot; the object-first flow is the better
  default and is hidden behind a ghost button.
- [x] 2.0.0 No search. To find the Eiffel Tower you pan and zoom by hand from wherever the map opened.
- [x] 2.0.0 Three unlabelled icon keys top right (calendar, crosshair, gear). The calendar opens "Trip", which a
  calendar does not say. 40 dp keys, under the 48 dp touch minimum.
- [x] 2.0.0 The map credit sits top left in a pill that looks like a key.
- [ ] No compass or north-up key, and no sign of which way is north once the map is turned.

### Map with one point
- [x] 2.0.0 Nothing on the map or the card says which point comes next except one sentence. The map cursor does not
  change. A wrong tap can only be fixed by Clear.
- [x] 2.0.0 "That is too close" is a toast: it vanishes, and it cannot be acted on.

### Answer card (two points)
- [x] 2.0.0 Title is the bearing and distance. That is a detail, not the headline.
- [x] 2.0.0 "Clear" is the most prominent key on the card (top right, raised). The destructive key should be the
  quietest.
- [x] 2.0.0 "Behind subject / Behind you" is jargon on a first screen, with no picture of what changes.
- [x] 2.0.0 Each answer row: badge, time, a grey line "Next · 6° above the horizon", an unlabelled bell. "Next" and
  "Low" are labels inside grey text; the difference between the two answers is the app's whole point and it is
  the least visible thing in the row.
- [x] 2.0.0 Tapping an answer does nothing. It should show that moment (map + viewfinder).
- [x] 2.0.0 No countdown ("in 19 hours"), no light quality (golden hour, blue hour, twilight), no weather hint.
- [x] 2.0.0 "Where to stand" appears again at the bottom of Times as a different, smaller feature (two rows, "Move
  here") from the full "Where to stand" list. Two things with one name.
- [x] 2.0.0 Footnote carries two unrelated facts: "Drag a point on the map to move it · times in Etc/UTC". The time
  zone is the phone's, not the place's: planning Paris from Riyadh gives Riyadh times with a raw zone id.
- [x] 2.0.0 The card height is capped at 42% and scrolls inside itself. A scroller inside a card over a map is three
  nested gesture surfaces.

### Day tab
- [x] 2.0.0 Chart is good but small; the hint "Drag along the day" is the only clue it can be touched.
- [x] 2.0.0 Day arrows move one day at a time. No way to jump to a date (a full moon next month is 30 presses).
- [ ] Ticks where a body is on the line cannot be tapped.

### Frame tab (the viewfinder)
- [x] 2.0.0 **Size.** 250 dp high inside a card capped at 42% of the screen; in landscape 3:2 it is about a third of
  the screen width at phone size. It should be the screen.
- [x] 2.0.0 **The sun is a 3 dp dot** at wide lenses, drawn under a 22 dp "Sun" pill that covers the sky around it.
  At 35 mm the label is eight times the size of the thing it names.
- [x] 2.0.0 **The subject is a blue dot** labelled "Subject" on a black strip. There is no silhouette, no height, no
  sense of the photograph. The thing the user wants to see (sun behind tower) is not shown.
- [x] 2.0.0 **Ground is a flat black band**, same at every distance. No haze, no depth, no horizon glow where the sun
  is.
- [x] 2.0.0 **Sky is a vertical gradient only.** The sun's side of the sky is not brighter; no stars at night; the
  moon has no earthshine; no golden or blue hour naming.
- [x] 2.0.0 **Gestures are invisible and unscaled.** Sideways drag = time at 2 minutes per dp with no scale, no
  ticks and no snap, so the sun jumps. Up/down drag = tilt, which fights the card's own scrolling. Pinch,
  the gesture every camera uses for zoom, does nothing; the lens is seven chips in a sideways scroller below.
- [x] 2.0.0 **No path.** You cannot see where the sun came from or is going, so you cannot tell whether dragging
  left or right brings it to the subject.
- [x] 2.0.0 **No "on the line" moment.** When the body crosses the subject nothing happens: no snap, no mark, no
  buzz. The time label is a line of text below the lens chips.
- [x] 2.0.0 "Sun on the line" and "Moon on the line" are chips that look like the lens chips above them.
- [ ] Only the camera's forward view. "Behind you" mode still shows the subject view with the sun out of frame.
- [x] 2.0.0 Rule-of-thirds grid is always on and is brighter than the sun at wide lenses.

### Where to stand (object first)
- [x] 2.0.0 Four rows of controls (end, light, distance, list) before the first result on a card capped at 46%.
  One and a half results are visible.
- [x] 2.0.0 Distance is four fixed chips. The user cannot say "from that bridge": dragging the ghost pin along
  the line to set the distance is not possible.
- [x] 2.0.0 The seven spots are shown one at a time on the map. Showing all of them as a fan around the object would
  let the user pick the one that falls on a street or a hill.
- [x] 2.0.0 "of the object" in the row, "Subject" on the map: two words for one idea.
- [x] 2.0.0 "Stand here" jumps to the Frame tab; the list is gone and there is no way back to it but Clear.

### Trip
- [x] 2.0.0 A separate full screen reached by a calendar icon. To add a line you must go back to the map, draw it,
  come back, press "Add the line on the map". Three screen changes.
- [x] 2.0.0 Lines can only be saved inside a Trip. There are no saved places or saved lines on their own ("my
  favourite spots").
- [x] 2.0.0 "Remove" is a raised button on every line, heavier than the line's name.
- [ ] "− 3 days +" stepper for length, a button for the first day: two controls for one range. A date-range
  pick is one control.
- [x] 2.0.0 Moments cannot be tapped to see them on the map or in the viewfinder; cannot be shared or added to the
  calendar.

### Settings, tutorial
- [x] 2.0.0 Tutorial teaches three taps and nothing else (not drag, not Frame, not Where to stand, not the bell).
- [ ] Settings has Theme, Accent, Map look, How it works. Missing: units (m / ft), 12/24 h, default lens,
  camera sensor (crop factor), reminder lead time (30 min is fixed).

## Controls: every one, and what to do with it

| Control today | Problem | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Tap map = place / move nearest point | Ambiguous third tap, no precision | Tap places only while a "placing" bar says which point. Afterwards tap selects; **long-press** drops or moves a point; **drag** a marker moves it with a magnifier loupe |
| Drag marker | Not discoverable | Markers get a grab ring on first show and lift with a shadow; haptic tick on pick-up and drop |
| Bottom card, fixed | Covers map, too small for views | A real **bottom sheet with three stops** (peek: the answer in one line; half; full). Drag the handle or fling |
| Chip rows for everything | One sign, four meanings | **Tabs** = underlined text tabs. **Either/or** = a two-part segmented switch. **Filters** = chips. **Values** (distance, lens) = a slider or pinch |
| "Behind subject / Behind you" chips | Jargon | A **flip key on the line itself** on the map (a small sun badge at the end of the line; tap to move it to the other end), plus the segmented switch in the sheet |
| "Clear" raised button | Loudest key is destructive | Overflow menu on the sheet: Clear, Save line, Share. Undo stays as a snackbar |
| Lens chips | Seven chips, scroll | **Pinch** in the viewfinder, with a lens ruler that snaps to 14, 24, 35, 50, 85, 200, 400 |
| Frame drag sideways = time | Unscaled, invisible | A **time ruler** under the view with hour ticks, day marks and magnets at "on the line", sunrise, sunset; drag the ruler or the sky |
| Frame drag up/down = tilt | Fights scroll | Kept in full-screen viewfinder only (nothing to scroll there); double-tap resets |
| Day arrows | One day per press | Arrows stay; tapping the date opens a **date picker**; fling the chart sideways for next/previous day |
| Bell icon | Unlabelled; 30 min fixed | Stays, gains a long-press for lead time (10, 30, 60 min, the evening before) |
| Toasts | Vanish, no action | Snackbars with an action (Undo, Zoom in) |
| Top-right icon trio | Unlabelled, small, wrong icon | Search bar across the top; locate and layers as map keys on the right edge (48 dp); Saved and Settings in the bar |
| New: **two-finger rotate** | none | Turns the map; a compass key appears and resets north |
| New: **double-tap an answer** | none | Opens the viewfinder at that moment |
| New: **hold the phone up** | none | In the viewfinder, a "Live" key ties the view to the phone's compass and tilt so you can sweep the real sky on location (no camera needed) |

## Missing key features (most useful first)

1. **Search for a place** (name or coordinates) for both You and Subject.
2. **Subject height.** With a height (or picked from a short list: person, tree, building, tower, peak) the
   viewfinder can draw a silhouette to scale and the answer can say "sun touches the top at 16:02". Today the
   subject is assumed to be a point on the horizon.
3. **Full-screen viewfinder** with pinch zoom, time ruler, sun and moon paths, and the on-the-line moment.
4. **Light quality**: golden hour, blue hour, twilight bands on the Day chart, the time ruler and each answer.
5. **Pick a date.** "When does it line up in June?" Start the search from any day, not only now.
6. **Time zone of the place**, shown as a city name, with the phone's time beside it when they differ.
7. **Saved lines** on their own, with Trip as a filter over them rather than the only home.
8. **Share / add to calendar** for a moment (text + map link, or a calendar entry).
9. **All spots at once** in Where to stand, and dragging the distance.
10. **Live compass mode** in the viewfinder (above).
11. Terrain height between You and Subject (is the subject even visible? does a hill hide the low sun?).
    Needs an elevation source; free ones exist (Open-Elevation, Open-Meteo) but need a network call.
12. Weather / cloud cover for the moment. Was a non-goal on 2026-11-14; listed for the owner to decide.
13. Units, 12/24 h, sensor size, reminder lead time in Settings.
14. Arabic and right-to-left (in the design language, not done).

## Visual cues and icons

- [x] 2.0.0 No hierarchy in the sheet: bearing 24 sp bold, answers 16 sp. Reverse it.
- [x] 2.0.0 Sun = amber, moon = violet is used well in badges but not on the map line, the chart ticks or the
  viewfinder labels consistently. Make it one rule everywhere.
- [x] 2.0.0 The line on the map is teal (the accent). The design language keeps the accent for actions; the line
  should be route green → route blue, as the icon is.
- [x] 2.0.0 The direction to the sun is a dotted line to an orange dot with no label; nothing says "the sun will be
  here at 3:19".
- [x] 2.0.0 Trip icon is a calendar; should be the route/bookmark icon from the CrossPaths set.
- [ ] No motion that explains: the answer appears by replacing the card. The sheet should rise, the sun badge
  should travel along its path to the line.
- [x] 2.0.0 No haptics anywhere.
- [ ] Touch targets under 48 dp: top icon keys (40), chips (about 30), small bells.
- [ ] Text 12.5 sp grey footnotes carry needed facts (time zone, how to move a point).

## Process (presses to the first useful result)

Today, knowing the subject: open → "Pick the object first" → pan/zoom to find it → tap → read four chip rows →
pick a row → "Stand here" → lands in Frame with no list. Seven steps, two of them reading.

Proposed: open → type the place (or tap the map) → sheet rises with **the next good light, where to stand,
and a picture of it**. Three steps. Everything else (other lights, distance, lens, trip) is one level down.

## Status after 2.0.0 (2026-11-14)

Done in 2.0.0: everything marked **[x] 2.0.0** above, the control table (except two-finger rotate and the bell's
long-press), and missing features 1 to 8 and 10. Feature 2 is done as a height you set (the Frame draws the subject
to scale); "the sun touches the top at" as a worked-out time is not. Feature 9: the spots are all on the map and
the distance is a slider; the ghost pin itself cannot be dragged yet.

Still open:
- Turning the map with two fingers and a north key (needs touch tests on a phone: markers and labels must stay upright).
- Ticks on the Day chart cannot be tapped.
- "Behind you": the Frame says the light comes from behind, but there is no look-back view.
- Trip length is still a stepper beside a date key.
- Settings: units, 12/24 h, sensor size, reminder lead time.
- Motion: the sheet glides between heights, the pin drops; the answer itself still appears without a transition.
- Chips are 36 dp tall (48 dp is the rule); footnotes still carry the time-zone note.
- Terrain height, weather, Arabic and right-to-left.
- Not checked on a phone: drag feel, pinch, fling of the ruler, Live compass, the keyboard in Search. The screens
  were pressed through in Robolectric only.
